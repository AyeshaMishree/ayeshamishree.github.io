Mental Health Technology

Alongside systems engineering work, a sustained thread runs through Ayesha's degree: using AI and software engineering to make mental health support more accessible, affordable, and trustworthy, particularly in resource-constrained, stigma-sensitive contexts like Pakistan. That work has taken shape as EmoCare, her idea for one of Pakistan's first integrated AI and therapist mental health platforms, recognition at a university exhibition and a national science fair, and a research paper now under review at a Scopus-indexed journal.

The Project

EmoCare, Smart Mental Health App with AI & Therapist Support

A three-tier platform, Android client, ASP.NET Core backend, Python FastAPI AI microservice, combining a two-stage AI pipeline (BERT emotion classification, followed by an LLM-generated CBT-style response) with verified therapist booking, secure Stripe payment, and a built-in crisis-safety branch.

What the paper covers

The paper situates EmoCare against three bodies of work: conversational AI for mental health, digital health platforms with therapist marketplaces (such as BetterHelp), and transformer-based emotion classification using hosted LLMs as empathetic-response generators. It argues that most existing mental health chatbots remain AI-only, lacking verified therapist consultation, secure payment handling, or formal crisis-escalation, and positions EmoCare's two-stage pipeline as a direct response to that gap.

The Stage-A classifier was fine-tuned on the publicly available Sentiment Analysis for Mental Health dataset (Kaggle, by Suchintika Sarkar), labelled across seven mental health statuses: Normal, Depression, Suicidal, Anxiety, Stress, Bi-Polar, and Personality Disorder. Stage B routes the classified state through OpenRouter to openai/gpt-oss-120b:free for CBT-aligned response generation. Supporting infrastructure includes Stripe webhook signature verification, Stripe Checkout, Firebase Firestore, ASP.NET Core, and FastAPI.
